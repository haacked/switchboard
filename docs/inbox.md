# The Inbox

Each session that needs you, or that Claude can continue, gets one item: a decision card with a title that says what you do, a kind (`decision`, `question`, `approval`, `review`, `action`, or `info`), a summary of usually two to three lines, options, and callouts. `sb triage` lists them in the order to go through them, and `sb next` prints the first. The same item is `triage` in `sb ls --json` and `sb get --json`, with a `source`, in this order of precedence:

- `card`: a held permission prompt or `AskUserQuestion` question. Allow and Deny, or the question's options.
- `session`: the card that the session wrote for its current turn with `sb summary set` (see [Session summaries](#session-summaries)). When a PR rule decides the state after the turn, such as a new conflict, the rule's reason leads the callouts, and the rule's options follow the session's.
- `rule`: the reason that a PR rule, a wait, or a follow-up gives, such as "Merge main in: #1204 conflicts with main". A PR rule that knows the next step adds its options and callouts with no model call: Merge and Acknowledge for a PR that is ready to merge, Request review for a PR that nobody reviewed and GitHub asks nobody to review, Merge main for a conflict, which asks the session to merge the base branch in rather than rebase, because a session in auto mode can refuse the force push that a rebase needs, and one option each for a retarget, requested changes, a Trunk removal, a failed Depot run, and an approved PR in a Trunk repo.
- `summary`: when a turn's prose reply decides the session's state and the session wrote no card, sbd runs `claude -p` with Haiku, no tools, no MCP servers, and no saved session, under the session's own account, and records a `triage` event. That can happen after the turn ends, as when a background job outlasts the turn or a PR rule stops applying. Each run of sbd calls it once per turn, so a restart retries a failed call. Two calls run at a time, each with a 60 s timeout. When the reply asks you to make several decisions, the summary has one line per decision, up to 10, in the reply's order and with the answer the reply recommends, and the kind is `decision`. A list of finished work is not a list of decisions. When the reply asks you to make two or more separate decisions, the summary also has `decisions`, one for each with its own options (see [Decision lists](#decision-lists)), and no `options`. Each decision's options come from the reply: the answer it recommends and each alternative it names, or Yes and No for a yes-or-no question or a proposal. A decision for which the reply names fewer than two answers, and that is neither, is left out. Its options can only send a message or take typed text. sbd skips the call, and records `triage` with `skipped`, when the account's quota is spent (see `sb usage`) or the session had `triage.perHour` calls in the last hour.
- `fallback`: the reply's closing section, with no options, until the summary arrives or when it fails or is skipped. When the reply's last list has at most one paragraph after it, the section is the line before the list, one line for each of the first 10 items, and that paragraph. An item that names a recommendation keeps its first sentence and the sentence that recommends. Every word comes from the reply, and the kind is the one that the reply's last paragraph gives, so a list of changes is not shown as decisions. Otherwise the section is the reply's last paragraph.

The order is: a held prompt or question; a decision, question, or approval; PR actions; finished work to review; Claude can continue. The oldest comes first in each group.

Every item has the same three sections in the same order, whatever its kind:

- **Summary:** only the context you need to understand the options and pick one: the summary lines, and the callouts. A fact is in it only if you would misjudge or not understand an option without it. The three-line limit, the Haiku prompt, and `sb summary help` all state this. The Haiku summary leads with what the session needs from you and the answer it recommends, or with what is finished when the reply asks nothing. A short reply, at most 1,200 characters and 20 lines, replaces a `summary` or `fallback` item's summary, and the title, callouts, options, and drafts stay. A rule item whose reply asks something leads with "The session asked: …", and a short reply shows under its summary as Latest reply. When the reply offers something with a yes-or-no answer, Yes and No come before the rule's own options, and Yes is the main option. A long reply, a session card's reply, and the reply of an item with decisions show behind Read full reply, which opens the reply in place. A held prompt or question, an operator report, and a rule item whose reply asks nothing leave the reply to Details.
- **Details:** everything else, collapsed until you open it: the tool call that a prompt asks about, a group message's text and targets, the part of the transcript that the item came from, the PR and its checks, and waits.
- **Action:** the buttons, and the decisions of a decision list: the options, Allow and Deny, Merge or Enqueue, Request review, Send and Cancel for a group message, a held question's answers, and Acknowledge.

Details sits above Action, so you can see that more context is there before you pick an option. Its transcript part shows the message that the item came from and Claude's text and tool calls before it, oldest first: the 5 steps before it, with each tool call on one line. A permission prompt or a question comes from its tool call, a reply item from the reply that its summary read, and a session card from the newest step. The item's own step has an accent bar, and the box starts scrolled to it. Show more reads 15 steps, then 30, then 30 more each time, up to 200. Open in transcript shows a chat's whole conversation, or another session's terminal. When Switchboard can't find the item's place in the transcript, the box is titled Latest steps and says so, and shows the newest steps. When the item didn't come from the conversation, such as a PR rule, a wait, or an operator report, the newest steps go last in Details, in a closed group titled Latest steps in the conversation that says so. When sbd can't read the transcript, Details shows the session's last reply in its place. The newest events show only after `defaults write <app bundle id> showsRecentEvents -bool true`.

Details starts open in two cases. A group message opens it, because its Summary names only the number of targets and the group, so you read the text and the targets before you send it. A permission prompt opens it when its Summary shows less than the whole call. Its Summary shows a Bash command up to 200 characters on one line. A longer command, a command with a line break, or a tool such as Write whose Summary names only a file, opens Details with the whole call, so you never allow a call that the Inbox shows in part.

`sb ls --json`, `sb get --json`, `sb triage --json`, `sb next --json`, and the MCP `triage` tool carry the sections as `triage.message`, beside the item's own fields:

```json
{
  "title": "Approve `touch x`", "kind": "approval", "source": "card", "ref": "card:3",
  "summary": "Claude asks to run `touch x`.", "options": [], "callouts": [], "multiSelect": false, "decisions": [],
  "message": {
    "summary": {"text": "Claude asks to run `touch x`.", "callouts": []},
    "action": {"options": [{"label": "Allow", "action": "allow", "recommended": true}, {"label": "Deny", "action": "deny"}], "multiSelect": false, "primary": 0, "decisions": []},
    "details": {"openByDefault": false, "toolInput": {"tool": "Bash", "text": "touch x"}, "lastReply": "…"}
  }
}
```

`message.summary` is `{text, callouts, drafts}`. Each draft is `{number, label?, text, replyTo?, post}`, where `number` is its 1-based position in the card and `post` says what its Post button may do (see [Session summaries](#session-summaries)). `message.review` is only on a Ready to merge item: the "Before you merge" section as `{approvals, noReviewRequired, detailsRead, requestsRead, threads, testGaps, codeOwnerGap, pendingReviewers, changesRequestedBy, amber}`. `amber` names the rows that need a look (`approval`, `threads`, `reviewers`, `testPlan`), in that order. `threads` is `{total, read, open, shown}`, where `shown` holds the first 10 open threads, each with the numbers of the card's drafts that reply in it as `drafts`. `requestsRead` is false while sbd hasn't read the review requests or the code owner requests, which makes `reviewers` amber. `codeOwnerGap` is `{"waiting": [[owners]]}` or `{"requested": [logins]}`. `sb triage` prints it with each row marked `[amber]` or `[ok]`. `message.action` is `{options, multiSelect, primary, decisions}`, where `primary` is the index of the main option, or null when no option is the main one, and `decisions` is the item's decision list (see [Decision lists](#decision-lists)), empty when it has none. `message.details` always has `openByDefault` and has only the parts that exist: `lastReply`, `toolInput` (`{tool, text}`), `pullRequest` (`{line, title, url}`), `waits`, `notes`, and `targets` (`name (id)` for each session of a group message). The newest events are not in it: `sb events <session>` prints them. The item's own `summary`, `callouts`, `options`, `multiSelect`, and `decisions` hold the same values as `message`, for scripts that read them. `sb triage` prints the Summary, the Action, and a line that names the Details.

Each option has an action, and a click runs a request that sb already has: `send` sends the option's message, `reply` sends text you type, `merge` and `dismiss` do what `sb merge` and `sb merge --dismiss` do, `requestReview` does what `sb review <session> <reviewer>...` does, `wait` sets the option's wait, `done` marks the session done, and `allow`, `deny`, and `answer` answer a held prompt. `sb choose <session> <option>` does what a click does, and `--text` gives a reply option its text and a Request review option its reviewers, separated by commas or spaces. On a multi-select card, `sb choose <session> 1,3` names several options, separated by commas, and they go as one reply. Inside Claude Code, `sb choose` refuses to answer a prompt, to merge, or to request a review. `sb reply <session>` prints the session's last reply from its transcript. `sb context <session>` prints Claude's steps that lead up to the session's item, and marks the item's own step. `--steps N` reads N steps before it, 5 by default and at most 200, and `--json` prints `{session, ref, anchor, anchored, more, steps}`, where `anchor` is `tool`, `reply`, `end`, or `none`, `anchored` says whether the item's step was found, and each step is `{kind, text?, tool?, summary?, id?, t?}`, oldest first. sbd also starts every work session and group chat with `--append-system-prompt` text that asks it to put choices through `AskUserQuestion`, and to write a turn that ends with three or more decisions with `sb summary set` and `decisions` instead, but never a list of finished work, and to put a reply to a GitHub PR comment or review in `sb summary set` as a draft with `replyTo` instead of posting it, and to write a session it names as a link to the session's `url`. In `config.json`, `triage` sets `enabled` (false turns the summaries off), `model` (default `haiku`), `account`, `timeoutSeconds`, `perHour` (default 6), and `systemPrompt` (empty turns the nudge off). A `systemPrompt` of your own replaces the whole default, so it asks for no `replyTo` unless it says so. A Manager conversation gets only the request to put choices through `AskUserQuestion`, and the operator gets none of this text, whatever `systemPrompt` says.

In the app, the Inbox shows the list on the left, and `j` and `k` move through it while it has focus. The right side shows the selected session's message as three titled sections, Summary, Details, and Action. Summary holds the summary, the callouts, and the session's last reply in the cases listed above. An item raised by an overdue wait shows each wait in a panel under the Summary, with Clear wait, Check now, and Open PR. Details is collapsed and holds the tool call or group message, the transcript steps around the item with their text rendered as GitHub Flavored Markdown, the PR and its checks, and waits. Action holds the options. Markdown images show their alt text until you click them, so text that a model wrote can't make the app request a URL or read a path. A click loads a web image from its URL, or reads a file image from its path, up to 20 MB, and refuses anything that isn't a regular file. A GitHub alert, a quote whose first line is `[!NOTE]`, `[!TIP]`, `[!IMPORTANT]`, `[!WARNING]`, or `[!CAUTION]`, shows its kind's icon, title, and colored bar, as GitHub shows it. When the card has callouts and the pane is wide enough for each column to get at least 260 points, the summary takes the left 55 percent and the callouts the right 45 percent, in the card's order. Both columns start at the top, and the taller one sets the card's height. In a narrower pane, or on a card without callouts, the callouts stack under the summary, which takes the full width. Each option is a full-width box stacked under the summary and the callouts, with its label as the title and its detail under it. Long text wraps. A box has a neutral background and border, so it never reads as a callout. A click on a box with a chevron runs its option at once. When the option sends text that differs from its label, or does something else such as a merge, the box says so in a last line. The recommended option is filled with the accent color, and ⌘⇧Return clicks it. A reply option's box holds a text field and a Send button. On a multi-select card, each box that sends a message has a checkbox: tick the ones that apply, then click Submit, which shows the message it sends and stays off until something is ticked. There Submit takes ⌘⇧Return, and the recommended option is marked Recommended. Acknowledge is a box too, which says that it sends nothing. A held prompt shows its card in place of the options. A held question shows each option as a box: a click answers a question with one choice at once, a multi-select question has checkboxes, a card with several questions has radio buttons for each one-choice question, and Submit sends those answers. Allow and Deny, a group message's Send and Cancel, and Merge All (or Enqueue All) stay plain buttons. A field at the bottom messages the session itself. The Terminal button shows the session's terminals in place of the card.

A card whose item has two or more decisions (see [Decision lists](#decision-lists)) shows them one step at a time in its Action, above the options, if it has any, such as a PR rule's Merge. The options keep their buttons, and ⇧⌘Return stays with the step view. Its Summary shrinks to its first line, and its callouts move into Details. A strip of numbered dots, one per decision, sits above the step: an empty dot is unanswered, a filled dot is answered, a dashed dot is skipped, and the current step has an accent ring. Hovering a dot shows its decision's header, and a click goes to it. Review, at the end of the strip, lists every answer with an Edit button. The strip wraps to a second row in a narrow pane. Each step shows the decision's header and question, its context under "More from the reply", and one box per option, with a radio button, or a checkbox on a multi-select decision, and Recommended on the reply's recommended option. A pick on a one-choice decision goes to the next unanswered decision after 0.3 s, and a tick on a multi-select decision stays on its step. Other answer takes typed text, which replaces any option, and Skip for now leaves the decision open. Each step also has a thread, "Ask about this decision", where you ask the session about that decision before you answer (see `--ask` under [Decision lists](#decision-lists)). The thread shows the decision's questions with each reply as GitHub Flavored Markdown, "Claude is answering…" with a spinner while the session answers, and the error of a question that ended without a reply. Return in its field asks, and its caption says that the session answers in its own conversation. The thread sits beside the options when the step is at least 640 points wide, and under them otherwise. A decision's dot has a speech mark once you asked about it, and a spinner while the session answers. While a question waits for its answer, Ask, the options, Other answer's Answer, Skip for now, the recommendation button, Submit, and Discard are off, and the step says which decision's answer it waits for. The question field keeps its text until sbd accepts the question, and unsent text survives a restart of the app. Each answer goes to sbd at once, so it survives a restart of the app. The Other answer field keeps its text until sbd accepts it, and unsent text survives a restart of the app. The text belongs to that list, so a later list's decision with the same number starts empty. Back and Next sit under the card's scrolling body, so they stay in view, and sbd's refusals show above them. On Review, "Use the recommendation for N unanswered decisions" picks the recommended option of each unanswered decision that has one. Submit stays off until every decision has an answer or a skip, and shows the whole message that it sends. When the session took a turn since the list opened, Submit asks first inside the card. Discard answers asks first too. Once sbd accepts Submit, the card says that it sent your answers in place of the steps until the session's next prompt replaces the item, after a restart of the app too. When the session replied with new decisions while a list is open, a banner offers "Answer the new ones…", which discards the open answers after a confirmation, and "Finish these first". While the step view has focus and no text field does, `]` or → goes to the next step, `[` or ← to the previous one, `1` to `9` pick an option (or tick it on a multi-select decision), `o` focuses Other answer, `a` or `/` focuses the thread's field, `s` skips, and `r` goes to Review. ⇧⌘Return clicks Next, or Submit on Review. A click on the step view's background gives it focus, and Esc in the Other answer field or the thread's field returns focus to it.

## Session summaries

A session writes its Inbox card at the end of a turn that needs you, and sbd shows it until the session's next turn starts. `sb summary set` reads the card as JSON on stdin, for the session in `$SB_SESSION_ID` or the one it names:

```json
{
  "title": "Pick how to land #98",
  "summary": "Opened #98; CI is green.\nTwo review items need a call.",
  "options": [
    {"label": "Fix + merge", "detail": "Fixes items 3 and 5, then merges", "action": "send", "message": "Fix items 3 and 5, then merge.", "recommended": true},
    {"label": "Merge", "detail": "Merges as is", "action": "merge"},
    {"label": "Wait for CI", "action": "wait", "wait": {"kind": "ci", "target": "98"}},
    {"label": "Skip", "action": "done"},
    {"label": "Something else", "action": "reply", "placeholder": "What should it do?"}
  ],
  "callouts": [{"kind": "warning", "title": "Drops events_old", "body": "The migration deletes the table."}]
}
```

- `summary` is required. sbd keeps its first three lines, each up to 200 characters. `title` defaults to the first line.
- `options` holds up to 6 options. A session may name the actions `send` (with `message`), `reply` (with an optional `placeholder`), `merge`, `wait` (with a `wait` object that takes the fields of `sb wait`), and `done`. `recommended: true` makes an option the main one. The `label`, up to 80 characters, is the option's title, and `detail` says under it what the option does.
- `multiSelect: true` lets you tick any of the options and submit them together, as for which review items to fix. Every option of such a card must be a `send` option. A single pick sends its message. Several picks send one message that lists each picked option's message on its own line, after `- `, in the options' order.
- `callouts` holds up to 5 things to read before deciding, each with a `kind` (`warning`, `decision`, or `info`), a `title`, and a `body`.
- `drafts` holds up to 5 messages for you to send to someone else, each a string or `{label, text, replyTo}`. The Inbox shows each in full with a copy button. `replyTo` names the GitHub review comment, review, or PR conversation comment that the draft answers, as the `html_url` that `gh api` gives, such as `https://github.com/o/r/pull/42#discussion_r123`, up to 500 characters. sbd keeps it only from the session's own card, never from the Haiku summary, whose text can quote PR comments. It accepts `https://github.com/{o}/{r}/pull/{n}`, optionally with `/files` or `/changes`, and a fragment of `discussion_r<id>`, `r<id>`, `issuecomment-<id>`, or `pullrequestreview-<id>`, and nothing else. The draft's `post` is `{state, reason?, error?, target?}`: `copyOnly` with a reason when the URL doesn't parse, when sbd hasn't read the session's PR yet, when the URL names another PR than the session's, or when GitHub places the comment on another PR or sbd can't find a review thread's root; `checking` while sbd reads the comment with `gh api`; `unresolved` with gh's error, such as a 404 or a refused login, until `sb draft check <session> <n>` reads it again; and `ready` with a `target` of `{kind, repo, pr, id, root, author, path?, line?, excerpt, url}`, where `root` is the review thread's first comment. A draft without `replyTo` is `copyOnly` with no reason. `sb triage` prints each draft's number, its text, and a line with its state. `sb draft post <session> <n>` posts a `ready` draft as you with `gh api`, as a reply in a review comment's thread or as a new PR conversation comment for an issue comment or a review. It posts the draft's text, the text of `--text`, or stdin with `--text -`, without leading and trailing blank lines, and prints `Posted: <url>`. It posts only from a terminal outside every session: `sb` refuses inside Claude Code, and sbd refuses a client that runs below any session's pane or below sbd's tmux server, such as a window that a session opened, or whose process it can't read, and no card option or MCP tool posts. sbd also refuses a draft of an item that changed since `sb draft post` read it, a draft whose target is no longer on the session's PR, an empty text, a text with a NUL character, and a text over 10,000 characters. sbd records `draft_posting` before gh runs, and a `post` then moves through more states: `posting` while gh runs; `posted` with the comment's `url` and the posted `text`, after which sbd refuses the draft, and any later card's draft with the same text for the same thread, which shows the same post; `failed` with an `error` when GitHub refused the reply with a 4xx or gh has no login, after which the draft can post again; and `unknown` with an `error` after a timeout of 60 s, a 5xx, a network error, or an answer that names no comment, and when sbd restarted during the post. An `unknown` draft can't post until `sb draft check <session> <n>` lists the thread's comments and looks for one by gh's user with the posted text. A match makes the draft `posted`, and no match makes it `failed`. After a restart, sbd's tick looks once on its own. On the session's next prompt, sbd tells the session which drafts the user posted, with each URL and any edited text, so that it doesn't post them again. It tells each post once. In the app, a draft with `replyTo` on the session's own card is a field that starts with the session's text, under a line that names the comment it answers, such as "Replies to @ann on a.swift line 3: ‘NOT over AND’", with the author linked to the comment. Edit the text and click the draft's Post button, which goes through `draft-post` as `sb draft post` does. Post has no keyboard shortcut, ⌘⇧Return never presses it, and it stays off while the field is empty or while sbd reads the comment. Use the session's text puts the session's draft back. A refusal or a failed post shows its error under the field and keeps your text. While sbd posts, and while it can't tell whether GitHub got the reply, the field is read-only. Check again runs `draft-check`, and Open the thread opens the comment on GitHub. A posted draft shows the text that went out and a Posted to GitHub link. The app keeps your edit with the message drafts in `~/.sb/drafts`, so it survives a relaunch, and drops it once the draft is posted or the item no longer shows it.
- `decisions` holds up to 10 separate decisions for a turn that ends with two or more, which you answer one at a time (see [Decision lists](#decision-lists)). Each has a `number` (the reply's own, from 1, or the decision's position when the reply doesn't number it, each one different), a `header` up to 24 characters, a `question` up to 200 characters, an optional `context` up to 1,000 characters, `multiSelect`, and 2 to 5 `options`, each with a `label` up to 80 characters, an optional `detail` up to 200 characters, and `recommended: true` on at most one. A card with decisions and no options has the kind `decision`. A card left with one decision and no options, such as after a broken decision is dropped, offers that decision's options as its own options, each sending its label.

sbd drops an option with another action, an option of a multi-select card that isn't `send`, or a wait that `sb wait` would refuse, a decision that breaks the rules above, such as one with a single option, two recommended options, two options with the same label, or a number that another decision has, and a callout with no title or another kind, and `sb summary set` prints what it dropped and why. The card is data: sbd shows it and never runs anything from it, and a button only runs its action, which makes its own checks. A merge button, for one, merges only a PR that is ready to merge. `sb summary help` prints the format for a session to follow.

Every item has an Acknowledge button, and ⇧⌘A does the same. It is the main button, and ⌘⇧Return runs it, when the item offers no options and no decisions to step through and only tells you something, such as "the review runs in a child session; I'll pick up its fixes when it reports back". A Ready to merge card offers it as one of its options. Acknowledging marks the item as seen: the session leaves the Inbox and Needs you and shows in Waiting with "acknowledged" on its Board card. Nothing else changes, so no message is sent, no wait is cleared, and the session isn't marked done. A work session that never had a PR is the one exception: when its last reply asks nothing, acknowledging that reply ends the session, which reads Done with "finished without a PR; nothing to commit or push" once the poller finds its folder clean and its HEAD on a remote's default branch. A new prompt takes it out of Done. It comes back when a new turn starts, when its reason changes (a card, a question, a PR rule), when its PR gets a new head commit, or an hour after you acknowledged it (`recheckSeconds.acknowledged`). An item that comes back after the hour has the same reason followed by "(acknowledged 2026-11-12 08:50, time to recheck)". Acknowledge it again to hold it for another hour. `sb ack <session>` does the same from a script, and `sb ack --undo <session>` puts the item back. `sb ls` marks an acknowledged reason with "(acknowledged)", and `sb ls --json` sets `acknowledged: true`. The Manager can run `sb ack`, but a session can't acknowledge itself. The acknowledgment is an `ack` event in the session's log, so it survives an sbd restart.

Snooze holds a session's item out of the Inbox until a time you pick. Right-click an Inbox item, a sidebar row, or a Board card and choose Snooze, then 15 minutes, 1 hour, 3 hours, Tomorrow (09:00 the next day), Next Monday (09:00 on the next Monday, so a week away on a Monday), or Pick a Time…, which asks for a date and time. The snooze belongs to the session, not to one item, so it holds whatever item the session shows until it ends, through new replies and new turns. While it holds, the item leaves the Inbox, Needs you, the counts, the Dock badge, the menu bar, Next Needing You, and notifications, and the session shows in Waiting. The session keeps working, and nothing is sent to it. A moon and the end time mark the session's sidebar row and Board card, and Unsnooze in the same menus ends the snooze at once. When the snooze ends, the item comes back with "(snoozed until 2026-11-15 09:00)" after its title and notifies as usual. The note stays until you act on the session, by answering it, sending it a message, or acknowledging it, or until a new turn starts. Three things end a snooze early, and the note then says why, such as "(snoozed until 2026-11-15 09:00, woke early: a new prompt)": a new card, permission prompt, or question; a failed CI run on the PR, when its checks weren't failing just before (checks that were already failing when you snoozed count only once they stop failing and fail again); and the PR merging. Nothing else wakes it: not a new reply, even one that asks a question in its text, a new turn, a wait that resolves or goes overdue, a conflict, a review or a review comment, Ready to merge, a PR that closes without merging, the session reaching Done, or the end of an Inbox request hold. `sb snooze <session> --until WHEN` does the same from a script, where WHEN is anything that `sb wait --until` reads, such as `2h`, `2026-11-15`, `2026-11-15 09:00`, or an ISO 8601 time with a zone. `sb snooze <session> --for DURATION` takes only a duration, such as `30m`, and without either flag the snooze lasts an hour. `sb snooze` refuses both flags together, a time that has passed, the session that runs it, a session marked done, and the Manager, a group chat, or the operator. Snoozing a snoozed session replaces its end time. `sb unsnooze <session>` ends the snooze, and refuses a session that isn't snoozed. `sb ls` marks a snoozed reason with "(snoozed until …)", `sb get` prints the end time, and `sb ls --json` sets `snoozedUntil` (ms since the epoch) while the snooze holds and `snoozeNote` after it ends. The snooze is a `snooze` event in the session's log, so it survives an sbd restart.

Highlight marks the session that matters most. Right-click its sidebar row, Board card, or Inbox item and choose Highlight, and choose Remove Highlight in the same menus to take it off. Only a work session that isn't Done can be highlighted, not the Manager, a group chat, or the operator. A highlighted session gets an orange ring around its sidebar row, its Board card, and its Inbox item. The Inbox lists highlighted items first, in a Highlighted section above Needs you, in their usual order among themselves, and each keeps its state's dot. j, k, the item the Inbox shows first, `sb triage`, and the MCP `triage` tool follow the same order. The sidebar, the Board, and the menu bar keep their order. sbd stores the highlight on the session, so it survives an sbd restart and every window and script sees it. Snoozing or acknowledging a highlighted session still takes its item out of the Inbox, and the item comes back first when it returns. The highlight clears when the session reaches Done: you mark it done, its PR merges or closes with a clean folder, it finishes without a PR as Acknowledge describes, or its worktree is removed. Marking it not done or reopening the PR doesn't bring the highlight back. A merged PR whose folder still has uncommitted changes is in Needs you, not Done, so it keeps its ring until the folder is clean. The Board shows that card in its Done footer, where cards have no context menu, so remove its highlight from the sidebar row, the Inbox item, or `sb highlight --clear`. `sb highlight <session>` highlights a session from a script, and `sb highlight <session> --clear` removes the highlight. It refuses the session that runs it, so a session can't raise its own priority. `sb ls` starts a highlighted row with `*`, `sb get` prints `highlighted: yes`, and `sb ls --json` and `sb get --json` set `"highlighted": true` on a highlighted session and leave the key out otherwise. The MCP `highlight` tool does the same for the Manager and other sessions, with `clear` set to true to remove it.

A message that answers a PR rule's item holds the session in Waiting while it works on the message. That covers a click on the rule's option, such as Merge main, a message typed in the Inbox's field while the item shows, and the Board card's merge-main button. sbd records a `request` event with the rule's reason and the PR's head commit, and from then on the session shows in Waiting with a reason such as "you asked at 2026-11-13 09:12: merge main in; #412 conflicts with main; 2 background jobs running" and "requested" on its Board card. Without the hold, the rule would put the item back in the Inbox at the end of every turn, even while the session waits on a background job to finish before it can do what you asked. The hold lasts while the rule gives the same reason at the same head commit. A push that resolves the rule, or any other change to the rule's reason, ends it, and the next rule decides. The hold also ends, and the item comes back with the rule's reason and a note, when the session stalls: when a turn that a background result started (a finished background job or subagent) ends without starting new background work, or when a turn ends with no background job or subagent left. sbd waits two GitHub poll intervals (`github.pollSeconds`, 60 s by default) after such a turn ends, so it reads a push that the turn made before it calls the turn a stall. The note reads "(you asked at 2026-11-13 09:12; the session stopped without doing it)". A last reply that asks a question ends the hold with "it asked a question", and an hour after the message (`recheckSeconds.requested`) the hold ends with "time to recheck". A message from another session, such as the Manager, or from `sb send` holds nothing. `sb ack --undo <session>` ends the hold, and `sb ls --json` sets `requested: true` while it lasts.

## Decision lists

An item with two or more `decisions`, from the session's card or the model's summary, can be answered one decision at a time. `sb decide <session>` lists the decisions, each with its options and your answer so far. `sb decide <session> <n> <option>` answers decision n with an option, by number or label, and on a multi-select decision, options separated by commas go together. `--text "..."` answers with typed text, which replaces any option, and `--skip` leaves the decision open. Every form takes `--json`, which prints the list. The `decision-answer` request takes the `ref` of the list that the caller saw, and refuses an answer when the list that the answer would go to has another ref: before the first answer, that is the current item's ref, and after it, the open list's. `sb decide` reads the list and passes its ref.

The first answer copies the item's decisions into a `decisions_opened` event, and each answer is a `decision_answered` event, so the answers survive a restart of sbd and the app. From then on the item shows the copy, and `sb get --json` has the list as `decisions`. A later card, rule, or turn doesn't replace the answers. When a later item has decisions of its own, the list keeps them as `newer`. A turn that starts after the first answer, such as a prompt you type in the terminal, marks the list `stale`. These events don't change the session's state and don't start a turn.

`sb decide <session> --submit` sends one message that answers every decision in order:

```
My decisions on the 3 questions in your last reply:
1. Which database should sbd use? Postgres (your recommendation).
2. What should the new note be called? Call it task
3. Which tiles show the icon? Not decided. Don't act on it yet. Ask me again later.
```

A chosen option sends its label, with "(your recommendation)" when the reply recommended it, a typed answer goes as typed, and a skip says that the decision is open. Submit refuses while a decision has no answer, and refuses a stale list unless you add `--stale`. It sends the message as `sb send` does, except that a stopped session starts without the message as its prompt and gets it as a paste after it starts. It records the send's `sent` event and `decisions_submitted`, with the message and the list's ref, in one transaction, so the log keeps both or neither, and nothing reaches claude before that transaction commits. When `sbd` can't store them, it refuses the submit and sends nothing. Until a new item replaces the one whose decisions it sent, `sb get --json` sets `decisionsSent: true`, and sbd refuses to list, answer, or submit those decisions again. While the send runs, sbd refuses a second submit, an answer, and a discard of the same list. `sb decide <session> --discard` drops the answers and sends nothing.

`sb get --json` reports how the submitted message travels in `decisionsDelivery`: `sending` until `sbd` records that the paste succeeded, then `delivered`. When `sbd` restarts while it sends the message, it pastes a message whose paste had not started, and checks the transcript for one whose paste had started (see [Sessions](sessions.md)). When the transcript doesn't show it, the delivery is `unknown`, and the Inbox card says that `sbd` restarted while it was sending the answers, so it can't tell whether the session got them, and offers Send again. `sb decide <session> --resend`, and the `decision-resend` request that Send again makes, send the same message once more. They refuse unless the delivery is `unknown`, so a second resend refuses while the first is on its way. A second `--submit` stays refused.

`sb decide <session> --ask <n> "<question>"` asks the session about decision n before you answer it. sbd sends the session this message, as sbd and not as you:

```
Question about decision 2 (Name) in your last reply, before I decide: why not B?

Answer only this question, in text. Don't change files, run commands that change anything, or start work. Don't write `sb summary set` or ask with AskUserQuestion this turn. I'll send all my decisions together when I'm done.
```

The turn that this message starts is a clarifying turn. Its reply goes under decision n, and `sb decide <session>` prints each question and its reply under its decision. With `--json` the list has them as `exchanges`, each `{askId, number, text, reply, error, pending}`, where `error` says why a question ended without a reply. While the message is on its way, during the turn, and after its Stop, the session keeps the state, reason, `since`, and Inbox item that it had when you asked. The Dock badge doesn't drop, no banner fires, its parent hears nothing, the list doesn't go stale, and the reply gets no summary. This lasts until you discard the list or a turn makes it stale. After a submit it lasts until the session's next prompt, so the card still says that it sent your answers and Resend still works. The session keeps the question and its answer in its conversation, so it knows what you asked when your decisions arrive. `sb decide --ask` waits for the reply and prints it, for up to `--timeout` seconds (600 by default), and `--json` prints `{askId, reply}`. `--no-wait` prints the question's id instead, or `{askId}` with `--json`. It refuses inside Claude Code, because a question to a session is yours to ask. The `decision-ask` request takes `{session, ref, number, text}` and returns `{askId}`.

sbd refuses a question about a list whose ref differs from the one you saw, a list that is stale or that you already submitted, a number that the list doesn't have, an empty question, a session with a held card, a paused session, a session whose turn runs or that has a message on its way, and a second question while one is pending. The first question opens the list, as the first answer does. While a question is pending, sbd refuses to answer, submit, or discard the list, and during the clarifying turn `sb summary set` refuses. A prompt that is not the question, such as one you type in the terminal, makes the list stale as any turn does, and when it comes during the clarifying turn, it ends the question with an error. When it comes after sbd started to paste the question and the paste lands, it ends the question with "The question changed before it reached the session. Ask again.", because text that you type in the terminal during the paste becomes part of the prompt that claude gets. Before the paste starts, while the question waits in the queue, such a prompt leaves the question pending, and so does a paste that fails and puts the question back in the queue. A turn that ends without a Stop ends the question with an error too: Claude Code's idle prompt, a StopFailure, an Escape that sbd finds, a prompt that the skill check blocks, a card answer that interrupts the turn or denies without a message, or claude exiting. A subagent's end of the turn doesn't end the question, and the main session's end of the turn after it still does. A question whose prompt never arrives ends with an error too: when claude stops or exits first, or when sbd restarts during the question's paste and its delivery becomes `unknown`. Its message can still reach the session later, and then it counts as a prompt you typed. The questions are events in the session's log, so they survive a restart of sbd.
