# Waits, pauses, schedules, and notes

## Waits

A wait says what a session waits on. Its kind is `ci`, `review`, `bot-review`, `approval`, `queue`, `merge`, or `deploy` for a PR, `session` for another session, `date` for a time, or `manual`. `sb wait <session> <note>` sets a manual wait, which stays until you clear it, or until its recheck time moves the session back to Needs you: `--until` when you give it, and a day later otherwise. sbd resolves `date`, `session`, and `manual` waits itself. A `session` wait resolves with "it did" when the other session reaches the state, "it was removed" when `sb rm` removes it, "it reached Done first" when it reaches Done and the wait wanted another state, and "it went stale" when it goes Stale (no activity for 2 days) and the wait wanted another state. Stale is how sbd tells that a session was abandoned. The GitHub poller resolves `ci` (checks passed or failed, or a Depot job failed on the head commit of a run that was not cancelled), `review` and `bot-review` (a review submitted after the wait was set), `approval`, `merge`, and `queue` (the PR merged or closed, or trunk or Trunk's comments show that Trunk merged it or removed it from the queue; the comments work without a trunk login), on the session's own PR or on the PR named by `--target`. The poller also resolves `deploy` waits (`--env dev`, `prod-us`, `prod-eu`, or `prod`, the default, for both regions). Without `--service`, a PostHog/posthog PR's wait resolves when its deploy comment says Deployed in every named environment. With `--service`, sbd reads that service's `state/<service>.yaml` in PostHog/charts every 2 min, compares the commit it runs with the PR's merge commit, and reads the sync job of the charts run that deploys it; the wait resolves when the sync succeeds, or with what went wrong when it fails or when the service doesn't build from the PR's repository. sbd can't read ArgoCD yet, so a deploy wait on a PostHog/charts PR, or on any other repository's PR without a service, resolves when the PR merges and says so. Any wait also resolves with `sb wait <session> --resolve "<what happened>"`. A resolved wait resumes the session with a message that says what happened. With `--then notify`, it moves the session to Needs you instead. When the session's folder is gone, the wait resolves without resuming the session, and its `wait-resolved` event in `sb events --json` says why in `error`. Every wait except a `date` wait and a manual wait gets a recheck time when it is set, which `sb get --json` shows as `recheck`. A manual wait set without `--until` gets its recheck time as its `until`. A wait past its recheck time stays open, and the session moves to Needs you with a reason such as "CI on #1204: still open after 2h". When the wait resolves later, it runs its `--then` as usual. Clear it or acknowledge the item to take the session out of Needs you. The Inbox item shows each such wait in a panel between the Summary and the Drafts: what it waits on, who set it and how long ago, when its recheck is due, what happens when it resolves, its note, and its target's state now. For a PR, that is the PR's state, checks, review decision, and pending review requests, with each team named by its slug, and for a `review` or `approval` wait with a reviewer, whether GitHub still requests that review. A team reviewer matches GitHub's request by its slug. For a `session` wait it is the other session's state, or that it was removed. A `deploy` wait shows its PR and says that sbd checks the deploy on its own. Clear wait drops the wait. Check now reads the PR from GitHub again, past sbd's cache, and the wait itself resolves at sbd's next poll. Open PR opens the PR. When a wait names a PR number without a repository, and the session has neither a PR nor a GitHub repository, the panel says that sbd can't tell which PR the wait is on. So does a wait on the session's own PR while the session has none. The recheck times are under `recheckSeconds` in [Settings](settings.md). A session with an open wait stops after `idleStopSeconds` idle, like any other. A session view lists each open wait in the panel beside its terminals, with who set it, when, and what happens when it resolves. Inside a session, `sb wait --kind ci` needs no session and waits on the session's own PR. A `ci` wait on the session's own PR records the commit that the session's branch points at. It resolves on one read of the PR's checks when the PR's head is that commit, the read comes at least half a poll interval (15 s by default) after the wait was set, and every GitHub Actions run of that commit has finished. sbd reads the Actions runs only for such a wait. Otherwise it needs two reads that agree, so a status that reports before CI starts can't resolve it early. `sb help` lists the flags.

## Pausing a session

`sb pause <session>` stops the session's claude and keeps it stopped. Use it instead of `sb stop` to free CPU or to hold work: a stopped session moves to Needs you and starts again on the next message, and a paused one does neither. Nothing starts a paused session or types into it. Every message to it waits until it resumes: `sb send`, a message from the Manager, a resolved wait's message, and the answer to a deferred card. `sb send` says that it held the message. A paused session shows in Waiting with a reason such as "Paused by Manager: free CPU; resumes when #1190 merges", which `sb get` and `sb ls --json` show too. It leaves the Inbox and auto-continue, and its claude no longer counts toward the live cap, so the next queued start takes its slot. `--reason` says why, and `sb get` shows it on a `paused:` line with who paused the session, when, and how many messages wait. For a session that is not paused, `sb get` shows a `queued:` line when messages wait in sbd's queue, such as one queued for a claude that exited before it started, which the next start delivers. `sb resume <session>` starts it again. When the session's folder is gone, `sb resume` still ends the pause, starts nothing, and reports the error. Claude gets `--message` from the resume, else the `--message` of the pause, else a note that says who paused it, and then the messages that waited, in the order they arrived. `--until` resumes the session on its own when a condition is met: a time (`2h`, `2026-11-15 09:00`), a PR that merges (`#123`, `pr:owner/name#123`, or a PR URL), or another session that reaches a state (`session:<name>[=<state>]`, Done by default). The session's own waits keep resolving while it is paused, and their messages wait with the rest. `sb pause <group>` pauses each session in the group that has started, isn't marked done, and isn't paused, and keeps the pause that a session already has. It prints one line for each session in the group, which says `paused`, `already paused`, `skipped`, or `failed` with the error. It refuses an `--until` that names a session in the same group. `sb resume <group>` resumes each paused session in the group, each with its own message unless you give `--message`. A ref that names a session and also a group is refused, so use the session's id or `--group <group>`. A group has no paused flag of its own, so a session that joins the group later isn't paused. Claude can pause a work session but not a Manager conversation or a group chat, and a session can't pause itself.

## Schedules

A schedule sends a prompt to a session on an interval. `sb schedule <session> --every <interval> <prompt>` adds one. It needs the session even inside a session, where Claude writes `sb schedule $SB_SESSION_ID …`. The interval is one number and one unit, `s`, `m`, `h`, `d`, or `w`, such as `90s`, `30m`, or `2d`. The shortest is `1m`. A compound form such as `1h30m` is refused with a hint to write `90m`. A session holds at most 10 schedules, and sbd refuses a second schedule on a session with the same prompt, ignoring white space at the ends, with the id of the one it already has. sbd stores them with the session, so they survive restarts of sbd, the app, and claude. The first run comes one interval after the schedule is added. sbd sends the prompt as is, so a slash command such as `/audit-waiting` runs as one. A run that comes due while the session is busy (a turn running, an open card, or a message that is queued or not yet delivered) waits until the session is free and then runs once, so a schedule never stacks prompts. A message queued for a stopped claude does not hold a run back, because the run starts claude and that start delivers the queued message before the run's prompt. The next run comes one interval after that run. The due times that passed while the run waited, or while sbd was down, do not run, and the run counts them as skipped. A stopped session resumes with the prompt, as a resolved `resume` wait resumes it. A run that fails, as when the session's folder is gone, records the error and waits for the next interval. A session marked done keeps its schedules and runs none of them until `sb undone`. A paused session runs none of them until `sb resume`, and a run that came due during the pause runs once after the resume message and the held messages. `sb schedule pause <id>` stops a schedule, and `sb schedule resume <id>` starts it again with its next run one interval away. `sb schedule edit <id> [--every <interval>] [<prompt>]` changes a schedule's interval, its prompt, or both, and keeps who added it. A new interval moves the next run to one interval from now when that is sooner, so a shorter interval never waits out the old one and a longer one never delays a run that was due sooner. A schedule that is due stays due, a paused one keeps its next run until resume sets it, and a prompt-only edit keeps the next run. `sb schedule run <id>` makes a schedule due now and sends nothing itself: the next tick runs it, so a busy session gets the prompt once, when it is free, and a stopped one resumes with it. The run counts nothing as skipped unless the session stays busy for more than one interval, and the next run comes one interval after it. Run now refuses a paused schedule ("Resume it first") and a session marked done, paused, or being removed, because those run no schedules. A session on a host whose link is down takes the request, and the run waits for the link. `sb rm` removes a session's schedules with the session. Each schedule keeps its last 10 runs, newest first, with each run's time, outcome (`pasted`, `resumed`, `queued`, or `failed`), skipped count, and error. `sb schedule ls [<session>]` lists the schedules, the next due first, with each one's prompt, interval, status, and last result, and `sb get` shows a session's. The status is one of `active` (`next in 12m`), `due` (`due 3m ago, runs when the session is free`), `overdue`, `failed` (the last run failed, until a run succeeds), `held` (`held: the session is marked done`, paused, or being removed), and `paused`. A due schedule turns `overdue` once it has waited one interval, at least 10 minutes and at most an hour. Every form takes `--json`. The rows that the `sb schedule` forms print carry `status` (`state` and `text`), and each schedule in the JSON carries its `runs`. A run that sent its prompt carries `sent`, the id of its `sent` event. A session view lists its schedules in the panel beside its terminals, between the waits and the notes, each with its status and buttons to pause or resume it, run it now, edit it, and delete it. Delete… asks first, because the prompt is lost. Run Now is gray, with the reason in its tooltip, for a paused schedule and for a session marked done, paused, or being removed. The status line turns the attention color for a `failed` or `overdue` schedule. The views of the Manager, a group chat, and the operator show a line such as "2 schedules · next in 12m" that opens the same list. The Loops view (⌘4) lists the schedules of every session that the filter shows, failed and overdue first, with the session as a link, the same status and buttons, and the selected loop's whole prompt and recent runs on the right. Its menus sort the loops by status or by session name, with the ones that need attention still on top, and show one session's loops. When the filter hides every session that has a loop, the view says how many loops it hides and offers Show All. The Loops button in the toolbar shows how many loops are failed or overdue. New Loop…, in the Loops view and in the File menu, adds a loop: pick its session, its interval, and its prompt. The session is the operator unless you pick another, and sbd creates the operator when there is none. A session that hasn't started, such as a follow-up waiting to start, isn't offered. Edit… changes a loop's interval, its prompt, or both, as `sb schedule edit` does. Save stays gray while the interval can't be read or the prompt is empty, and the sheet stays open with sbd's reason when sbd refuses the loop.

A top-up schedule keeps a number of work sessions running on a repository's open issues, and wakes the operator only when a session needs it. `sb schedule Operator --every 30m --topup --group G --repo owner/name --target N --template-file F --rubric-file F2 [<prompt>]` adds one, as does `add_schedule` with a `topup` object that carries `group`, `repo`, `target`, `template`, and `rubric`. `--group` takes the group's name or id, `--target` is 1 to 20, the template file holds the prompt of each session started from an issue, with the placeholders `{url}`, `{title}`, `{number}`, `{repo}`, and `{body}` that an issue preset takes (see [Issues](follow-ups-and-issues.md#issues)), and the rubric file says how to rank the issues, most important first. `--topup` needs all five other flags, and they need `--topup`. Without a prompt, sbd stores "Work queue tick for <group> in <repo>: merge and clean up what is finished, then top up to <target> working sessions from the open issues and follow-ups, most important first, by the rubric and template below." Only a Manager conversation, the operator, or you in a terminal can add a top-up or edit one, with the same check on the client's process ancestry and `caller` that an armed wait gets, so sbd refuses a work session and a group chat. `sb schedule edit` changes only the interval and the prompt, so to change the group, repository, target, template, or rubric, remove the schedule and add it again. sbd refuses a second top-up on a session for the same group and repository. At each due time sbd looks at the group's work sessions that have started. A session is in the repository when its checkout's origin is the repository, or when its PR, its issue link, or its follow-up is in the repository, as for a session in a fork's checkout. A session in the repository counts as active unless it is marked done, paused, being removed, in Done, Needs you, or not started, or its PR merged. A session whose start waits behind the live cap with a message for that start counts in any state, unless it is marked done, paused, being removed, or its PR merged, because the message runs once a slot frees. A queued start with no message waiting doesn't count. sbd sends the prompt instead of acting on its own when a session's repository is one it hasn't read yet or couldn't read, when a session is on a host whose link is down or whose sessions sbd hasn't read again since the link came back, when a session in the repository is Ready to merge and no wait will merge it, or when a session in the repository is Done or its PR merged and no wait will remove it. A wait held by a session marked done doesn't count. Otherwise, when the group has its target, sbd records the check and sends nothing, and the next run that sends shows how many due times sbd checked this way, as in `pasted 5m ago · 3 gated`. When the group is short, sbd lists the repository's open issues and open PRs with `gh` and leaves out each issue that a session or an open PR works on: by the issue number in its branch or in the branch it started on, its issue link, or the issues its PR closes. A session whose repository sbd hasn't read yet covers each issue whose number is in its branch. The follow-ups that wait to start in the group and the repository are candidates too, unless they are marked done or wait for a date or a merge that hasn't come. sbd skips an issue that neither you nor one of the team's people (`sb issues team`) wrote, because the sessions start unattended, and it trusts no author until it knows your GitHub login. `claude -p` with no tools, on `operator.model` (default `sonnet`) under `triage.account` or else the operator's account, picks at most as many candidates as the group lacks, by the rubric. It reads each title, body, and follow-up prompt inside tags, as data. `operator.topupTimeoutSeconds` (default 120) limits how long the pick may run. For each pick, sbd checks again that the schedule is still there and not paused, that the group is still short, that no session needs attention and sbd can still read every session, that no session covers the issue, and that the follow-up can still start, then starts it: an issue as Issues' Start does, on `<branchPrefix><number>-<slug>` in the gate's group, with the template as its prompt, and a follow-up with its own prompt. sbd arms `--state done --then rm-worktree` on each one, held by the operator. On each tick, when a started session that isn't marked done has an open PR in a repository that `operator.mergeRepos` lists and no merge wait, sbd arms `--state ready_to_merge --then merge` on it. sbd arms it once per session, so a merge wait that the operator clears isn't armed again. With the list empty, a started session that reaches Ready to merge makes sbd send the prompt, so the operator handles it. When a session starts, sbd records one report on the operator, such as "Top-up: started fix-cache (#12), inbox-dupes (#15); skipped #9: needs a design decision", and sends the operator nothing. A schedule runs one top-up at a time, so a due time or `sb schedule run` during one starts nothing more. The checks and the top-up run while the operator is busy, but they run nothing while its session is marked done, paused, or being removed. When sbd sends the prompt, it waits for the session to be free as any schedule does, and puts a status in front, such as "Work queue: 3/5 active sessions in owner/name." with a line for each session that needs attention, and the rubric and the template after it. When the issue or PR list, the pick, or its answer fails, or every pick fails to start, sbd sends the same message with "The top-up failed: <reason>" as its first line. When the session is busy then, sbd records the failed check, sends nothing, and tries again at the next due time. When the schedule is gone or paused by then, or its session is marked done, paused, or being removed, sbd sends and records nothing. `sb schedule ls` and `sb get` add the last check to the schedule's line, such as `topup Infra owner/name 3/5 · checked 2m ago: gated`, where the result is `gated`, `started`, `prompted`, or `failed` with its error, and `topup Infra owner/name · target 5 · never checked` before the first. The Loops view and the session's schedule list show each run's gated count and, after the last run, the last check, such as `top-up 5/5 · checked 2m ago: gated`. In the JSON, the schedule's `gate` carries the last check as `last`, with the active count, the target, the sessions started, and each skipped candidate with its reason. sbd records a `schedule-gated` event for a gated check, and a `schedule-topup` event for a top-up with the picks, the sessions started, and the skips.

To move a work queue from a plain loop to a top-up: write the rubric file and the template file, add the top-up schedule, optionally add the repository to `operator.mergeRepos` so that sbd arms the merges of the sessions it starts, and then remove the old loop with `sb schedule rm <id>`, so the operator doesn't run both.

## Notes and to-dos

A note is plain, or a to-do: a task for you that Claude never runs, such as telling someone in Slack that a fix shipped. `sb note <session> --todo <text>` adds one through the `note` request with `kind: todo` (`note` is the default), as does a session's Claude with `sb note $SB_SESSION_ID --todo …`, and the Manager or a chat with `add_note` and `todo`. A group's notes are always plain: `group-note` and `group-add` refuse `kind: todo` with "To-dos belong on sessions, not groups." Only you resolve a to-do. `sb note <session> --resolve <id>` sends `resolve-note`, which refuses `by: claude` and any note that isn't an open to-do, and `sb note --resolve`, `sb rm --resolve`, and `sb followup rm --resolve` refuse inside Claude Code. A session with an open to-do can't be removed until you resolve it: `rm` and `followup-rm` refuse before anything stops, and name each to-do, unless their `resolve` list names exactly the session's open to-dos, which the delete then resolves. A to-do added while `rm` stops the panes keeps the session, and `rm` says so after it removes the worktree. Delete all Done skips such a session with its to-do's text and resolves nothing. `sb note --rm` refuses an open to-do. `sb cleanup` removes the worktree and keeps the session and its to-dos. Each resolution writes a row to the `resolved_todos` table in sbd's database, which stays after the session is deleted, and `resolved-todos` (`limit`, default 50) lists the rows. `sb note <session>` lists open to-dos first, then plain notes, then resolved to-dos, `sb get` marks each to-do, and `sb ls` shows the count, such as `[2 to-dos]`, after a session's reason. In the app, a note icon shows on the sidebar row, Board card, Inbox row, and Inbox card of each session with notes, and in every session view's header, where it adds the first note. The icon is dim when the session has only plain notes or resolved to-dos, gray when it has an open to-do, and orange when a Done session has one. It shows the number of open to-dos when there are two or more. Its tooltip shows the first open to-do. A click lists the open to-dos, each with who added it, when, and a Resolve button, then the plain notes, then the resolved to-dos under a collapsed row. Each note's text shows as GitHub Flavored Markdown. A field at the bottom adds a note, or a to-do when To-do is checked. A session view also shows a panel on the right of its terminals with the session's open waits, the same notes list, its artifacts, and its links. Each link shows its kind's icon, its title, who added it and when, Open, and Remove. A link added without a title shows a short form instead of its URL, such as `Slack thread in C0123ABCD`, `Linear ENG-12`, or the URL's host and path. Remove deletes a link that you or Claude added and hides a link that sbd found, as `sb link rm` does. The session's hover card says how many links it has. The header's sidebar button hides the panel and shows it again, for every session. The panel also hides, and the button is off, while the view is too narrow to hold it beside the terminals at their minimum widths, as in the Inbox's Terminal view. The trash confirmation and the Remove dialogs of a session, a follow-up, and a docket row list the open to-dos as they were when you clicked, and the button reads "Resolve 1 To-do and Delete". The request resolves only the to-dos that the dialog listed, so a to-do added after that makes sbd refuse, and the error banner shows the refusal.
